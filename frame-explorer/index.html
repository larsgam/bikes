CHARACTER PROFILE — FOUR INDEPENDENT AXES

Each axis draws on its own data source and expresses one property: where the frame puts your body (Position), how it behaves (Handling), what the structure weighs (Lightness), how fast the shape is (Aero). Filled dot = measured · hollow dot + ~ = estimated · ? = no data · dashed outline = at least one axis not fully measured.
How the axes are computed
Position (0 upright → 100 low & stretched): 100 − stack-to-reach ratio normalised against the road spectrum 1.40–1.75.
Handling (0 calm → 100 quick): mean of inverted trail (50–75), wheelbase (970–1100), chainstay (405–450) and BB drop (60–85).
Lightness (0 heavy → 100 climbing-light): inverted frame weight against 650–1100 g. Requires a verified weight.
Aero (0 round tubes → 100 fully optimised): inverted wind-tunnel wattage against 200–230 W (Tour Magazin protocol, 45 km/h) where an independent measurement exists; otherwise a design assessment, always flagged as estimated.
Normalisation ranges are fixed, so scores stay stable as frames are added. An endurance bike shows up as the pattern low-Position + low-Handling — not as its own axis.

Bars show relative differences (not zero-based, so small angle differences stay visible). Geometry comes from manufacturer charts; trail is derived from head tube angle + fork rake + wheel radius (700c + 30 mm tyre); weights and tyre clearance come from the spec sheets above.
